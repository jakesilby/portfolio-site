CASE STUDY

Linking Documents & Prescriptions

Lives inside OnePulse Connect's Global Inline Drawer, the panel that surfaces a patient's documents and prescriptions inline during dispensing. The drawer itself is background context here.

Linked Documents connects prescriptions and medications to the documents that support them, so staff can attach an incoming document to an existing patient record instead of restarting intake, and the system can resolve exactly which document prints when more than one could. I designed the interaction model and its guardrails.

01

Problem

Two separate views had no way to talk to each other. During OnePulse Connect's dispensing workflow, staff work across a documents tab (authorization letters, EOBs, signed prescriptions, consent forms tied to a patient) and a prescription tab (prescription info and the patient's medication list, different objects with different data behind them), both inside the Global Inline Drawer. Nothing connected the two.

BEFORE/AFTER, STATIC — DOCUMENT CARD & PRESCRIPTION/MEDICATION CARD, EACH WITH ITS "LINKED" SECTION REMOVED (BUILD FROM REAL CARD COMPONENTS)

The cost showed up as real, specific workflow problems. When a doctor sent an ERX for a patient already set up in workflow, staff had no way to attach it to the existing record, they had to restart intake from scratch. A single document, an authorization letter covering two dosage variants of the same drug, for example, sometimes needed to apply to more than one medication, and the system had no way to express that. We scoped the fix properly in a working session with the team on June 5, 2026.

02

Why This Matters

Jillian, who works documents from the patient profile rather than an active workflow, described hunting for an approval letter after a medication had already been dispensed, with no reliable way to find it by medication name. Staff were already renaming files "DO NOT USE" as a workaround, since there was no way to deactivate a document without deleting it.

There was also a sharper, safety-adjacent problem underneath. Once a document could be linked to multiple medications, "Print Prescription" needed to resolve to exactly one document, even with several Rx Prescription-type documents on file. Left ambiguous, the system either can't act or a staff member has to guess, in a clinical dispensing context. That's not something this tool could leave loose.
